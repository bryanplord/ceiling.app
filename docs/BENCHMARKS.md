# Ceiling — age benchmarks (Oct 13, 2026)
Men 40–49 vs endurance athletes 40–49. Population figures are solid; athlete figures come from small or self-selected cohorts.

| Metric | Men 40–49 | Endurance athletes 40–49 | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| VO2max (ml/kg/min) | 10th 22.1, 50th 35.3, 90th 50.8 | 25th 48.3, 50th 54.3, 75th 60.2, 90th 65.2 | FRIEND 2022 (Kaminsky, Mayo Clin Proc); athlete test cohort n=385 (A Faster You) |
| VO2max, other | HUNT3 healthy men 47.2 ± 7.7; Cooper 95th 52.5 | Elite ~60–70+ (uncertain) | Loe 2013; Cooper Institute |
| Resting HR | mean ~65 (Fitbit men 40–49: 64.6), SD ~7.7 | mid 40s–low 50s overnight | Quer 2020; Deus 2019 |
| HRV rMSSD | WHOOP men ~45: middle half ~35–60 ms | ~50–90 (uncertain) | WHOOP, Oura, Nunan 2010. Device-dependent: own baseline matters more |
| Max HR | predicted 178 at 43; measured 184 ± 11 | same formula | Tanaka 2001; HUNT3. Not a fitness score |
| Sleep | 6.8 ± 0.8 h; ~30% of men under 7 h | 8 h+ to absorb training | Quer 2020; CDC NHIS |
| Training | ~47% of adults meet 150 min/week | 8–15 h/week | CDC NHIS; triathlon cohorts |
| Body fat | 50th 21.9%, 90th 14.9% (Cooper); DXA median 28.8% | 6–13% | Cooper Institute; ACE; NHANES |

- **Altitude:** VO2max measured at about 1,800 m reads roughly 9% lower than at sea level (Wehrlin & Hallén 2006: 6.3% per 1,000 m).
- **VO2max estimates:**
  - Uth–Sørensen 15.3 × HRmax / HRrest, ±4.5% with measured max. It assumes a seated morning resting HR, so overnight wearable RHR is raised by ~6 bpm first.
  - ACSM cycling: 10.8 × (best 5-min W/kg) + 7.
  - Garmin/Firstbeat: ~5% error vs lab.
